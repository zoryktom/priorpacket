# Competitive Landscape

## Market map

Prior authorization automation is crowded. PriorPacket should avoid claiming it replaces the big platforms. The startup should position as an evidence-completeness layer that can sit before or beside existing systems.

## Competitor groups

### Clearinghouse and network platforms

Availity offers end-to-end authorization workflows, supports CMS-mandated FHIR-based approaches, and aligns with Da Vinci CRD, DTR, and PAS. It positions around routing, payer-provider collaboration, and utilization management automation. Source: [Availity End-to-End Authorizations](https://www.availity.com/end-to-end-authorizations/).

Why PriorPacket can still exist:

- Availity is a network and enterprise platform.
- PriorPacket can be open-source, local-first, and evidence-focused.
- PriorPacket can integrate into workflows before submission.

### Revenue-cycle automation platforms

Experian Health offers prior authorization software with a knowledgebase of payer requirements, multiple payer connection types, AI routing, and automated back-end submission. Source: [Experian Health Authorizations](https://www.experian.com/healthcare/products/patient-access-registration/prior-authorization-software).

Infinx combines software, automation agents, and specialists for determination, initiation, follow-up, manual work, EMR updates, and documentation gathering. Source: [Infinx prior authorization automation](https://www.infinx.com/prior-authorization-solution-ai-and-automation/).

Why PriorPacket can still exist:

- These products often sell as end-to-end services.
- PriorPacket can win developers, informatics teams, and smaller specialty groups with transparent tooling.
- The wedge is packet quality and evidence-gap analytics, not staffing replacement.

### Payer/provider collaboration platforms

Cohere Health supports prior authorization workflows across medical services, procedures, and specialties and positions heavily around payer-provider collaboration. Source: [Cohere provider solutions](https://www.coherehealth.com/solutions/providers).

Why PriorPacket can still exist:

- Cohere is strongest when payers and providers are already on the same platform.
- PriorPacket can help providers prepare defensible evidence packets independent of payer platform adoption.

### Medication ePA platforms

CoverMyMeds is widely used for medication prior authorization and says its provider solution is available for medications and all plans at no cost to providers. It reports 900,000 providers using CoverMyMeds. Source: [CoverMyMeds provider solution](https://marketingbuilder.covermymeds.com/solutions/provider/).

Why PriorPacket should not start here:

- Rx prior auth already has strong incumbents.
- CMS-0057-F prior authorization API requirements discussed in the current rule exclude drugs.
- Medication PA is operationally different from medical-item and service PA.

### Touchless prior auth platforms

Rhyme says it processes more than 4 million prior authorizations for 83 large providers each year. Source: [Rhyme](https://www.getrhyme.com/).

Why PriorPacket can still exist:

- Rhyme is likely enterprise-first.
- PriorPacket can be the open, inspectable evidence layer for teams that need customization and transparency.

## Differentiation

PriorPacket should not say:

- "We automate prior auth end-to-end."
- "We replace Availity, Epic, Cohere, Infinx, Experian, or CoverMyMeds."
- "We guarantee approval."

PriorPacket should say:

- "We make the evidence packet inspectable before submission."
- "We turn payer policies into testable evidence checklists."
- "We show why a request is not ready."
- "We generate a packet staff can review, export, and attach."
- "We are FHIR-native and designed for the 2027 interoperability transition."

## Open-source advantage

Open source is useful because health systems do not blindly trust opaque AI in revenue-cycle workflows. A deterministic engine gives technical buyers a reason to inspect, test, fork, and validate the logic before procurement.

Commercial features should be built around the hard parts that open source users will not want to maintain:

- Payer policy monitoring.
- Policy-pack authoring and review.
- Enterprise deployment and support.
- EHR integration.
- Work queues.
- Analytics.
- Security paperwork.
